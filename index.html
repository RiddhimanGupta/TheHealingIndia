The Problem I Am Solving [cite: 5]

India's food delivery industry runs on the backs of over 12 million gig workers[cite: 6]. In 2023, 75,000 two-wheeler riders died on Indian roads — that is 9 deaths every single hour[cite: 8].

The pressure of platform-driven delivery deadlines creates a system where riders are algorithmically incentivised to speed and take risks[cite: 11]. THI aims to protect these riders, who are the most vulnerable people on Indian roads[cite: 7].

What THI Is [cite: 15]

THI is a lightweight Android background service that runs alongside Google Maps[cite: 16]. It does not replace Google Maps; it works silently in the background, providing legal protection, emergency response, and road intelligence without interrupting the rider's workflow[cite: 14, 17]. It requires no extra hardware, just the smartphone the rider already carries[cite: 18].

Core Features [cite: 21]

DriveLegal [cite: 22]

Provides subtle audio alerts when approaching known challan-prone zones[cite: 23, 24]. Riders can also check their challan status pulled from the Vahan database[cite: 26].

RoadWatch [cite: 28]

Uses the phone's accelerometer to detect potholes[cite: 29]. Confirmed reports are stored in a community database to build a verified hazard map[cite: 32].

RoadSoS [cite: 33]

Detects potential crashes using sudden drops in GPS speed and sharp accelerometer impacts[cite: 34, 35]. A 10-second alert window triggers [cite: 36]; if unresponsive, it sends emergency alerts to the nearest hospital and police station via the Overpass API[cite: 37].

The Rider Dashboard [cite: 39]

Designed to be glanced at between deliveries[cite: 41]:

  • Today's Route Map: A map overlay colour-coded by speed zone and challan risk[cite: 42].
  • Challan Zone Crossings: Logged time and location for personal evidence[cite: 43].
  • Road Hazards Reported: Verification status of bad road stretches[cite: 45].
